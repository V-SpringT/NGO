How We Work

Good intentions need a way of working.

Four commitments guide how we make decisions, build partnerships, and stay engaged over time. They are not rules — they are the discipline we hold ourselves to.

01

Listen locally.

The people closest to a challenge understand its context and possibilities better than anyone working from a distance.

Proposed question: Who is already doing the work, and how do they define the support they actually need?

02

Partner thoughtfully.

Durable progress depends on organizations with local trust and practical expertise.

Proposed question: Does this organization have the community trust and operational capacity to lead this effort?

03

Follow evidence.

Bring together data, lived experience, and continuous learning.

Proposed question: How will we know if this is working, and are we prepared to change course if it isn't?

04

Invest for the long term.

Stronger systems and community capacity take time and require sustained attention.

Proposed question: Are we committing enough time and patient resources for this partnership to build lasting capacity?

Our proposed operational loop

In practice, we hope these four principles would work together in a continuous cycle — not a linear process.

What this looks like in practice

We do not fund cold applications.

We build relationships before making grants. If you are doing work we are interested in, the path begins with a conversation — not a form.

We separate our role from the work.

Partner organizations lead the work. Our role is to provide patient support and useful resources — not to take credit or direct implementation.

We publish our learning.

What we learn from each area — including what surprised us and what did not work — is shared through our field notes and case studies.

We stay when it is hard.

Long-term commitment means being present through setbacks, not just successes. We try to be the kind of partner that does not disappear when things get difficult.
