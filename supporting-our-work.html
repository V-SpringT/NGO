Funding Approach

Supporting Our Work

We are a small private foundation. Our capacity to fund is finite. But we believe in the value of collaboration — whether through co-funding, knowledge-sharing, or partnership. Here is how you can be part of this work.

Engagement

How we collaborate and connect.

We welcome connections from individuals, organizations, and other funders who share our core values and are interested in exploring meaningful collaboration. The right form of engagement naturally depends on who you are and the unique perspective you bring to the table.

For foundations and institutional funders working within Vietnam's social sector, we are always open to conversations about shared learning, co-investment in specific partners, or even joint field visits. We do not pursue co-funding merely as a fundraising metric; rather, we seek it out when combining resources serves the local partner far better than solo funding ever could.

We are equally interested in connecting with knowledge partners and advisors—researchers, practitioners, and sector experts whose work intersects with our focus areas. If you are conducting rigorous, community-focused work on education access, ecosystem restoration, or health support in Vietnam, we would love to learn from your insights.

For individual supporters looking for ways to contribute directly to the causes we highlight, we encourage you to deeply explore the stories we share. By reading about the organizations doing this vital work on the ground, you can find direct pathways to support them and champion their efforts.

Collaboration Principles

Our approach to co-funding.

We believe that co-funding works best when all parties share a mutual understanding of how funding relationships should respectfully operate. When we enter into a collaboration, we do so with a few guiding principles in mind.

First and foremost, our arrangements must be partner-centered. Collaborative funding should simplify life for the organization doing the work, rather than creating overlapping reporting burdens or administrative headaches. We actively work with our peers to align requirements whenever possible.

Transparency and non-directive support are also critical. We are committed to sharing what we know about a partner with any co-funder, provided the partner consents to that sharing. In return, we ask for the same openness. Furthermore, neither funder should ever use joint funding as leverage to redirect a partner's overarching strategy. We are there to fund the work they have designed, not to create confusion about who is in charge.

Finally, we strive to be entirely honest about our limits. We are clear about what we can and cannot commit to over the long term, and we expect our peers to operate with the same candor. No funder should imply more support or broader influence than they can realistically deliver.

The best thing we can do is stay honest, stay engaged, and learn from the people who actually know how to do the work.
