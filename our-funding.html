How we start
What we fund

Four areas. Consistent criteria. Patient commitment.

We direct funding toward organizations working within our four focus areas. Across all of them, we look for the same things: strong local trust, clear operational capacity, honest self-assessment, and a genuine commitment to learning from the work.

Focus Area 1

Learning & Opportunity

Organizations building sustainable reading cultures, library networks, and learning environments — especially in underserved highland and rural communities.

Focus Area 2

Climate & Community Resilience

Organizations supporting ecosystem restoration, biodiversity protection, clean water access, and long-term community-led environmental stewardship.

Focus Area 3

Health & Well-being

Organizations providing sustained, relationship-based support to families navigating rare diseases, mental health challenges, or complex care needs.

Focus Area 4 — Exploratory

Arts & Civic Life

This area is still in development. We are building understanding before making any formal commitments. No grants are active here yet.

Our process

How decisions get made.

We do not accept unsolicited grant applications. All of our funding relationships begin with a conversation. Here is how a relationship typically develops.

1

We identify or are introduced to an organization.

We discover potential partners through field work, peer networks, and trusted referrals. Occasionally, an organization working in our focus areas will reach out directly — those introductions are welcomed, though they do not guarantee funding consideration.

2

We have a conversation.

Before anything else, we want to understand an organization's mission, approach, and what they are learning. We listen first. We ask questions. We may visit in person if the relationship has depth.

3

We assess alignment and capacity.

We consider: Does this work align with our focus areas? Does this organization have the operational capacity to use support well? Is there a clear and honest theory of change? Are they learning from what they do?

4

We make a commitment — usually multi-year.

Our grants are typically structured as multi-year commitments. We prefer this because meaningful progress takes time and because we want partners to plan with stability. Grants include an expectation of regular, honest dialogue about progress.

5

We learn together and publish what we find.

We share what we learn from partnerships — including what worked, what did not, and what surprised us. We do not publish partner information without consent.

What we look for

Partner criteria.

We look for organizations that meet these conditions:

  • Registered nonprofit, social enterprise, or equivalent legal structure
  • Operating in Vietnam or with direct programming in Vietnam
  • Working within one of our four focus areas
  • Demonstrated track record of work in community (any scale)
  • Clear, honest accounting of program costs and outcomes
  • Willingness to share learning openly — including failures
  • Local community trust — not just local presence
What we do not fund

Outside our scope.

We are not the right funder for every good organization. We do not fund:

  • Individual scholarships or personal grants
  • Organizations based outside Vietnam (unless programming is directly in Vietnam)
  • Political campaigns, advocacy for specific political parties, or lobbying
  • Religious organizations for religious activities (we may fund non-sectarian programs)
  • Capital construction projects as a primary use of funds
  • Emergency relief or disaster response
  • Organizations that have not yet operated for at least one full year

"We are a small foundation with limited capacity. We are selective not because we doubt the value of work outside our scope — but because focusing well means saying no to many good things."
