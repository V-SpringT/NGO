Funding Approach

Funding Approach

How we would evaluate thoughtful support. We aim to be transparent about our thinking.

What we fund

Four areas. Consistent criteria. Patient commitment.

We direct our funding toward organizations working within our core focus areas. Across all of our partnerships, we look for the same foundational elements: strong local trust, clear operational capacity, honest self-assessment, and a genuine commitment to learning from the work being done.

Our funding efforts are largely concentrated on initiatives that foster learning and opportunity. We work closely with organizations that build sustainable reading cultures, develop library networks, and create welcoming learning environments, particularly in underserved highland and rural communities where access to resources is often limited.

We also recognize the critical importance of climate and community resilience. In this space, we stand alongside organizations dedicated to ecosystem restoration, biodiversity protection, and clean water access. We believe that long-term environmental stewardship is most effective when it is led by the local communities themselves.

In the realm of health and well-being, our focus shifts toward providing sustained, relationship-based support. We look for programs that help families navigate complex care needs, such as rare diseases or mental health challenges, ensuring they have the trusted information and connections they require.

Additionally, we are currently exploring the role of arts and civic life. While this area is still in its developmental phase and we have not yet made any formal grant commitments, we are actively building our understanding of how creative participation and accessible cultural spaces can enrich community life.

Our Approach

How we evaluate support

Rather than relying on a rigid, bureaucratic application process, we prefer to evaluate potential partnerships through open conversations and a few guiding principles.

When considering where to direct our support, we prioritize a deep sense of alignment. We look closely at whether the proposed work naturally intersects with our goals in learning, climate, health, or the arts. However, alignment alone is not enough; community trust is absolutely essential. We believe that lasting change only happens when an organization is deeply rooted in and trusted by the people it serves. The voices, needs, and experiences of the community must be at the very heart of their planning and execution.

We also value organizations that demonstrate genuine capacity alongside complete honesty. We look for teams that are capable of delivering on their promises, but who are equally comfortable discussing what they do not know and where past efforts may have fallen short. True progress requires acknowledging failure as much as celebrating success.

Ultimately, a strong commitment to learning is what we look for in every partner. We want to work with those who actively seek out evidence, adapt their methods based on daily realities, and generously share their insights to elevate the efforts of others in the field.
