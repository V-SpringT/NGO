H'Hen Nie: Empowering Highland Children to Conquer Knowledge

January 2, 2023 Dak Lak & Central Highlands Provinces H'Hen Nie Foundation

H'Hen Nie — Miss Universe Vietnam 2017, and the first woman of Ede ethnicity to win the title — has always been candid about her origins. She grew up in Buon Cham village, Ea Tiêu Commune, Cu Kuin District, Dak Lak Province, in a community where formal education was rare and girls were expected to farm, marry young, and repeat the cycle of poverty.

At her 2017 pageant appearance, she spoke plainly: "I want to fight for Ede women and girls so they can go to school, chase their dreams, and become who they truly want to be." Since winning the crown, she has worked tirelessly to make those words real.

"One day as Miss Universe, a lifetime as Miss Universe. That title changed my life — and I want to use it to change the lives of others." — H'Hen Nie

Programs and Impact

Over five years of charitable activity, H'Hen Nie has built a multi-pronged support system for highland children:

  • Friendly Libraries (Room to Read): 15 libraries built across Lam Dong, Dak Lak, Tra Vinh, Vinh Long — with a mobile library van serving the most remote communities.
  • Clean Wells for Villages: Providing safe drinking water to ethnic minority households previously reliant on contaminated sources.
  • School Construction: Building and renovating classrooms, including the Ta Mieu school in Dien Bien Province.
  • Scholarships: Donating 70% of her 2017 pageant prize; all of her 2018 Miss Universe prize money; and ongoing support via fundraising campaigns.
  • COVID-19 Orphan Sponsorship: Personally sponsoring children who lost parents to the pandemic, including young Huynh Tan Phat.

She also regularly visits schools in remote areas to share her personal story — from highland poverty to Top 5 at Miss Universe. In a Dan Sinh interview, she explained her philosophy:

"When I visit these communities and see the children, I see myself as a little girl — the same hunger for knowledge, the same dreams. Building these libraries means giving them the key to unlock a better future." — H'Hen Nie

Asked about the physical demands of volunteering in remote areas — the long travel, rough conditions, impact on her appearance — she simply laughed: "Helping others makes me understand the world around me better. And I come from the mountains. The mud doesn't scare me."

Source: Dan Sinh – Dan Tri
